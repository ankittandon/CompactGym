CompactGym

A gym for the moment an AI agent has to forget. It measures what a long-running agent loses when its history is squeezed to fit, and what that costs in money and waiting.

Motivation

Forgetting you can't see

Agents run out of room. To keep going they compact: summarize, trim or move the history aside. What gets dropped decides what the agent does next.

01

It is silent

Lose "don't email Jonas" and nothing errors. The agent keeps working, confidently.

02

It is delayed

The mistake shows up many turns later, far from the compaction that caused it.

03

It is priced

Summaries cost tokens, break the prompt cache and make the user wait. Quality is only half the bill.

CompactGym plants facts early in a 28-request session: a person to leave out, a date that moved, a reference number. It asks for them late. A rule-based verifier diffs the final mailbox and calendar, and a fact counts as forgotten only if the full-context agent got it right.

Any step can be snapshotted and replayed under a different compactor with identical seeds. The difference between branches is the effect of the compaction itself.

    Why the obvious fixes fail

    Three things we tried that did not work alone

    A

    Summarize it

    Forgets the least of the real methods (0.25), but costs 2.2× more and blocks the user for about two minutes per session.

    B

    Keep the log, let the agent search

    A popular idea: never summarize, retrieve exact text on demand. It forgot 1.33 facts per session, five times the summarizer. The agent called search about once per session. You cannot search for a rule you do not know you forgot.

    C

    Train on a quality score

    InfoLoss measures how much worse the model predicts the next turns after one compaction. Against real forgetting it points the wrong way, because one compaction is not the 17 that follow it.

    What worked

    Pin the rules. Truncate or retrieve the rest.

    Keep the user's standing instructions in a short pinned list that is always visible, then compact everything else cheaply. With the true facts pinned, both cheap methods forget less than the frontier summarizer.

    Corrections

    What we got wrong

    The buried-fact test did not test what we meant

    We expected facts that live only in a long email to hurt truncation. They did not, and our first explanation (agents re-reading the email) was a miscount: re-reads were 0 of 11. The likelier cause is our own prompt, which asked the agent to summarize the email, so it restated the fact in its own reply.

    Success differences are real when paired

    We first said success differences between methods were not significant. Paired by branch point, the frontier summarizer beats truncation by 0.08.

    Our first reward check was flawed

    We resumed some episodes with only two or three turns left, which made forgetting look rarer than it is.

    A memory-search bug understated retrieval

    Trailing punctuation broke keyword search, so "Jonas." never matched a search for "jonas". It is fixed and tested.

    Limits

    • Small samples: 12 to 14 branches per method.
    • The frontier method is an open model standing in for a closed one.
    • Budgets of 8K and 16K stand in for on-device windows and long sessions. Current models have far larger windows.
    • The environment is simulated, with short and explicit instructions.
    • Oracle pins are an upper bound. A trained pin extractor is not part of these results.
